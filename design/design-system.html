Colour

Three-tier system, not a palette

The rule that matters more than the hex codes: colour carries meaning, not decoration. Green especially — it's a promise, not a style choice.

Semantic green
#0B8F5A
Halal-status signals only. The Halal pill, the open-now dot, a confirmed caveat tick. Never a UI state, never "selected," never decoration.
Ornamental green
#0B6B45
The seal, primary CTAs. Close enough to read as "affirmative" without being the literal status colour.
Brass
#B8924E
Decorative only. The seal's ring, section rules, the quote mark. Never carries information.
Ink
#0F0F0F
Primary text, and selected/active UI states (filter chips, price pills). Being selected is a UI state, not a halal signal — this is what green must never be used for.
Link blue
#1F56BA
Genuine navigation only — Collections links, external evidence links. Not filter states, not general emphasis.
Amber
#B06005
Partial status, caveats worth knowing. Not alarming — a levelled note, not a warning.
Red
#C02929
A specific negative fact — "Alcohol served." Used sparingly, on the one chip that needs it, never as a page-level warning colour.
Chalk / bands
#FCFCFC · #FFFFFF
Base and alternating section fill. The whole site is white-first — colour is earned, not default.

Layout

Section rhythm

White and pale-grey ("band") sections alternate down every page — the only structural signal separating one section from the next. No section should ever need a border or shadow just to read as distinct; alternating fill already does that job.

WHITE — hero, verification, location
BAND (#FAFAFA) — overview, menu-was-here, similar spots
WHITE — footer sits on band, always

Voice

How the site talks, not just how it looks

  • No em-dashes, anywhere — not in visible copy, not in meta tags, not in structured data. It's a recognisable AI-writing tell now, and the whole pitch is that a human actually checked. Code comments are exempt, they're invisible to users.
  • Dates over declarations. "Checked 3 August 2026" carries more trust than "Verified ✓" ever could, and doesn't need a page explaining how — the date does the work.
  • Never self-congratulatory. No "Verified by us" byline — stating you verified your own page, on your own page, tells a visitor nothing they didn't already assume.
  • Honest about placeholders. "Photos coming soon," "Map coming soon" — say plainly when something isn't real yet rather than dressing up a pattern as a photo.
